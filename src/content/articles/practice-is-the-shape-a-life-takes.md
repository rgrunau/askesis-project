Most of us imagine change as an event. There is the person we are now, the person we mean to become, and somewhere between them a decisive moment: the program started, the insight finally understood, the promise made with sufficient force.

It is an appealing picture. It is also rarely how a life changes.

Change is less like crossing a threshold than wearing a path. One pass leaves almost nothing. The next follows the faint suggestion of the first. Eventually the ground remembers where you have been.

## What repetition is doing

Every action is small enough to dismiss. A walk instead of another hour in the chair. One breath before answering in anger. Telling the truth when a more flattering version is available. None of these acts announces a transformation.

But each is evidence. Each makes the next similar act slightly more available.

This is why practice matters more than intention. Intention describes the person we would like to be. Practice rehearses that person into existence.

> Character is not waiting underneath the noise to be discovered. It is being formed by what we repeatedly do.

The body understands this without argument. Strength is accumulated resistance. Endurance is accumulated effort. Skill is accumulated attention. No single repetition is the thing, yet there is no thing without the repetitions.

The same is true of patience, courage, and honesty.

## Attention before correction

Practice does not begin with force. It begins with seeing.

A familiar feeling arises. It produces a familiar thought, which reaches for a familiar action. Usually the sequence is complete before we notice it happened. Mindfulness creates a small interruption—not peace, necessarily, but room.

In that room, inquiry becomes possible.

What am I assuming? What am I protecting? What would courage require here? The question does not guarantee a better response. It makes one available.

Then the work becomes physical again. Stand up. Speak plainly. Put the shoes on. Leave the room before saying the cruel thing. Return when you can tell the truth. The examined life still has to be lived through a body.

## No finish line

There is no final repetition after which patience is secured, attention cannot wander, or character no longer needs tending. This can sound exhausting if practice is understood as a project—another unfinished object demanding completion.

But a practice is not a project.

A project points toward the day it will be over. A practice gives shape to the day you are already in. Its value is not deferred until you become someone else.

The path is made by walking it. The mark is made by returning. The life is not waiting on the other side of the work.

The work is the shape the life takes.
